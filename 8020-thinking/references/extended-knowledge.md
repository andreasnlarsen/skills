## Part 1: Case Studies

### Rachel — The 20% Spike in Action
Managed branded womenswear for 8 years. Doubled sales, multiplied profits **15x**, achieved 50-60% return on capital with no new capital needed. Her spike: selecting merchandise with a commercial instinct — she assumes only 40% sells at full price and buys accordingly. She outsourced everything outside her spike. Koch: "She is a driven person, she is unusual, she adds a quite disproportionate amount of value." **Frameworks**: 20% Spike, Vital Few Analysis.

### Filofax — Pruning the Trivial Many
Koch and Robin Field rescued near-bankrupt Filofax in 1990. Strategy: concentrate on the vital few 20% of products and customers. Cut ~80% of SKUs. Result: turned a large loss into 15% net profit within 3 years. Volume sales quadrupled. Share price went from GBP 1 to GBP 320 (sold at 18x purchase price). **Frameworks**: Vital Few Analysis, Counterintuitive Principle #5 (pruning > adding).

### Belgo — Attributed Time-Compression Example
Belgian restaurant concept. Achieved 7-8 seatings per night vs the industry standard of 1-2, with BETTER service quality. The source attributes the result to faster, more focused service. This anecdote does not establish that shortening a deadline causes better quality. Extraordinary financial returns. **Frameworks**: Time Compression, Counterintuitive Principle #6 (speed + quality linked).

### LEK Consulting — Business Genes in Action
Founded 1983 by Koch and two partners. Mutated the BCG/Bain consulting gene: competitive analysis + M&A advisory. Staff, revenues, and profits grew at **100% per year** through the 1980s. 300+ staff by 1989. Key 80/20 findings inside LEK:
- M&A work: 22% of revenues, **87% of profits**
- Large projects: 21% of revenues, 56% of profits (46% return on sales vs <10% for others)
- Old clients: 26% of revenue, **84% of profits** (55% ROS). New clients: 31% net loss
- Koch was <4% of partnership but took 80% of profits
- Associate consultants (cheapest staff) were most profitable per billable hour
- "Lucky blunder": tried to hire 20 MBAs, got only 4 but accidentally hired 28 undergrads — who became the firm's identity

**Frameworks**: Business Genes, Vital Few Analysis, 20% Spike.

### The Consulting Gene Tree
- **McKinsey** (1926): Marvin Bower's gene = "consulting as a profession" (borrowed from law firms)
- **BCG** (1963): Bruce Henderson's mutation = strategy consulting (market research + financial analysis)
- **Bain & Co** (~1970): Bill Bain's mutation = CEO relationships + loyalty + implementation
- **LEK** (1983): Koch's mutation = competitive analysis + M&A advisory
Each generation mutated the prior gene, didn't create from scratch. **Framework**: Business Genes.

### Jamie Reeve — Personal Monopoly from Nothing
30-year-old, employee #105 in BBC hierarchy. Organized a trip for John Birt (BBC Director General) to Silicon Valley, July 1997. "Just John Birt, Bill Gates, and me... talking for two hours." BBC Online launched November 1997. Reached 10M users, 300M page impressions/month. Estimated worth $1-2B. One junior employee created more value than thousands of senior ones. **Frameworks**: Personal Monopoly, 20% Spike, Asymmetric Bets.

### Koch's Personal Investing — Asymmetric Bets + Compounding
The notes report a start near $500K in 1983 and later wealth near $1.6B, with less than a day per week spent on investments. The endpoint date, contributions, and withdrawals are not established here, so these figures do not establish an investment return. The earlier claim of this growth over about 30 years at 22% annually was inconsistent: without cash flows, $500K at 22% for 30 years is about $194.9M; reaching $1.6B over 30 years would require about 30.9% annually. These calculations correct the arithmetic, not the unverified biography. Three investments (Filofax, Belgo, MSI hotel company) comprised 20% of net worth but >80% of gains. The following are attributed historical investing rules, not instructions for a current portfolio:
1. Match investment philosophy to personality
2. Be proactive and unbalanced (concentrate, don't diversify)
3. Invest mainly in stock market
4. Invest for long term
5. Invest most when market is low
6. If can't beat market, track it (index funds)
7. Build on expertise and personal knowledge
8. Consider emerging markets
9. Cull loss makers — 15% stop-loss rule
10. Run your gains (never sell winners)

**Frameworks**: Asymmetric Bets, Compounding, Vital Few Analysis.

### Zoffany Hotels — Focus + Compounding
Koch provided capital. Share value went from GBP 1 to GBP 320. 10 hotels achieving 30-40% return on sales. Strategy: focus exclusively on 3-star hotels in medium-sized provincial towns. No expansion into other segments. **Frameworks**: Vital Few, Personal Monopoly.

### Apple — 90/10 Network Dominance
2007: Nokia, Samsung, Motorola, Sony Ericsson, LG had ~90% of worldwide mobile phone profits. By 2015: Apple grabbed **92% of all smartphone profits** in a much larger market. Four of five former leaders making losses. iTunes launched with 200K tracks at $0.99 each; predicted 1M downloads in 6 months, hit it in 6 days. Apple made 30% of all platform revenues. If exactly 20% of firms receive 80% of positive total profit, their average is 16 times the remaining firms' average: `(0.8 / 0.2) / (0.2 / 0.8)`. An exact 90/10 split gives 81; 99/1 gives 9,801, not infinity. These are conditional arithmetic ratios, not evidence of a real market distribution or future dominance. **Frameworks**: Compounding, Personal Monopoly, Vital Few.

### Anne Scheiber — Patient Compounding
Invested $5,000 in blue chips post-WWII. Worth $22 million by 1995 — about a 439,900% increase. Never changed strategy. **Framework**: Compounding.

### The Lazy Bosses
- **Bruce Henderson** (BCG): "Laziest person Koch knew." Stopped consulting 2 years into founding BCG. Only recruited and wrote "Perspectives" essays. Built a global firm.
- **Bill Bain**: "Second laziest." Co-inventor of growth-share matrix. Only attended monthly partner meetings and mentored. Others worked 60-70 hrs. Bill played tennis.
- **Jacques vs David**: Two consulting bosses. Jacques arrived 9-10am, left early, long lunches — but had first-rate mind, selected best consultants, started a more successful venture. David worked 8-8, less successful.
- **Bing Crosby**: First to pre-record radio broadcasts so he could play more golf.

**Framework**: Von Manstein Matrix, Counterintuitive Principle #1.

### Southwest Airlines — Value Innovation
Eliminated: meals, seat assignments, hub-and-spoke routing, interline baggage. Added: frequent flights, fun culture, low price, point-to-point routes. Result: profitable every year in a notoriously unprofitable industry. **Framework**: Value Innovation.

### IKEA — Value Innovation
Eliminated: delivery, assembly, sales staff assistance. Added: stylish Scandinavian design, self-service browsing experience, low price. Result: global furniture dominance. **Framework**: Value Innovation.

### Honda — Vital Few / Think Small
Entered US market with small motorcycles that Harley-Davidson and others dismissed. Created an entirely new market segment. Eventually moved upmarket from position of strength. **Frameworks**: Vital Few, Think Small (Route 1).

### Go-Gurt — Business Genes Mutation
Scott Lutz created yogurt in a tube — a simple mutation of existing product. Result: $100M+ revenues. Drove Yoplait past Dannon in US market. **Framework**: Business Genes.

#### The 7 Routes to New Ventures
1. **Think Small**: Dominate a neglected niche (Honda small motorbikes)
2. **Think Big**: Combine existing products/tech into something new (Apple personal computer, Bain Capital from Bain & Co.)
3. **Think Upmarket**: Premium for those who'll pay (Lexus: 2% of units, ~30% of profits)
4. **Think Mass Market**: Radical cost reduction to open new demand (Ford Model T, budget airlines)
5. **Value Innovation — More for Less**: Simultaneously ADD and SUBTRACT (IKEA, Southwest, Formule 1 hotels)
6. **Direct Distribution**: Cut middlemen (Dell: 6% cost-of-goods advantage)
7. **Cherry-Pick Highest Value-to-Cost Activities**: Only do the sweet spots of an industry

#### Happiness Islands + Achievement Islands
Identify your "happiness islands" — times of disproportionate joy. And your "achievement islands" — when you produce 10x results. Maximize both. Redirect time and energy toward these peaks.

#### The 8-Question Spike Finder
1. What do you most enjoy doing at work?
2. What do you think is unusual about you compared to your colleagues?
3. What have been your peak experiences at work?
4. What has given you the best results relative to the time spent?
5. What do people most compliment you on?
6. What do you find easy that other people find hard?
7. When are you in a state of flow?
8. Looking at your career, what threads run through your best achievements?

#### Diagnostic: Signs You're in the Trivial 80% of Time
Use these to IDENTIFY where time is going, then redirect toward the vital few:
1. Activities driven by other people's priorities
2. Activities done "because we've always done it this way"
3. Activities outside your spike
4. Activities you find draining
5. Activities constantly interrupted
6. Activities that have already taken twice as long as expected
7. Activities with unreliable or low-quality collaborators
8. Repetitive cycles with diminishing returns
9. Activities you can't connect to your core purpose
10. Status meetings

#### Top 10 Best Uses of Time (Koch's Time Oases)
1. Things that advance your overall purpose and meaning in life
2. Things you have always wanted to do (but never done yet)
3. Things already in the 20/80 relationship of time to results
4. Innovative ways that slash time required or multiply quality
5. Things other people say can't be done
6. Things others have done successfully in a different arena
7. Things which allow you to use your personal creativity
8. Worthwhile things you can easily delegate
9. Activities with great partners who use time eccentrically and effectively
10. Things where it is "now or never"

## Part 3: Supplementary Source Concepts

These are proposed lenses and source rhetoric. They do not establish universal business, psychological, or scientific facts.

### 5 Mega-Hints for the 80/20 Future
1. **Only work in networks** — network businesses generate most of the money
2. **Small size, very high growth** — join a company with <100 employees growing >30%/year (ideally <20, doubling annually)
3. **Only work for an 80/20 boss** — focuses on few things, rarely flustered, looks for outputs not inputs, leaves you alone when trusted
4. **Find your 80/20 idea** — behind every great company is an idea that delivers more for less
5. **Become joyfully, usefully unique** — the future rewards unique innovators, not hard workers or conformists

### Black Ducks (Koch's extension of Taleb's Black Swans)
Black swans are rare, unpredictable, history-changing events. Koch's addition: "Black ducks" are the smaller building blocks of black swans. If connected, they would have predicted the black swan. More actionable than trying to predict the unpredictable.

### Super-Connectors
Individuals who link many people across different clumps of society. "Most super-connectors are not famous, and many are shy or introverted." Their advantage: they get new information faster by bridging disconnected groups. Network society functions because of super-connectors.

### Territory vs Hierarchy (Steven Pressfield)
Two ways to define yourself. **Hierarchy**: defined by rank, authority, position. **Territory**: defined by what you create. "If you have your own territory, you can evade hierarchy and enjoy autonomy." Apply at every scale: career, product positioning, company strategy.

### The Subconscious Mind ("Hidden Friend")
The source uses a “hidden friend” metaphor for background thought and time away from a problem. The earlier assertion that Pierre Janet established that 92% of the brain operates subconsciously has no supporting evidence in these notes and must not be presented as neuroscience. Treat the practical suggestion to alternate focused work and space for reflection as a context-dependent hypothesis.

### Neuroplasticity and Happiness
"Perhaps the most important scientific discovery in terms of potential impact on human happiness." The brain rewires itself. Temperament is NOT destiny — happiness can be cultivated through deliberate practice and habit formation. Apply 80/20: which 20% of happiness practices produce 80% of wellbeing improvement?
