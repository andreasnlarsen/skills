# Contrasting evaluation cases

Use these cases to check decisions produced by the skill. The pass condition is a defensible action and causal explanation, not reciting the algorithm. They are synthetic reasoning checks, not evidence of improved real-world performance. A self-review must not be described as an independent evaluation.

| Case | Situation | Decision to look for | Failure to catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wrong proxy | A founder asks for an hourly report; the real decision is weekly, and the data updates daily. | Establish the decision and freshness need before building an hourly system; propose a suitable alternative with evidence. | Automating the requested cadence without examining its purpose, or silently overriding an explicit user choice. |
| Necessary function | A rarely opened audit log is the only record used to investigate annual disputed transactions. | Assess the rare dependency and preserve the function; challenge retention or implementation separately if justified. | Deleting because usage is low. |
| Restoration | A removed routing field causes an identifiable class of requests to go to the wrong team. | Restore it or supply a smaller adequate replacement and retain the learning. | Defending deletion to preserve a metric or blaming the operator. |
| Reversible omission | A daily scratch export has no consumer in the inspected workflow; its owner is uncertain whether an occasional use remains. A local trial is authorized, reversible, and can expose requests for the export. | Try a bounded omission with a useful observation period and restoration criterion, accounting for plausible rare use. | Requiring proof of no conceivable use before any experiment, or declaring the export permanently useless from current usage alone. |
| Deletion quota | A team has justified eight removals and restored none; someone demands a failed ninth removal to meet the 10% rule. | Examine the quality and breadth of the necessity tests; do not manufacture a failure to satisfy the number. | Treating restoration as a required output. |
| Hidden cost | Removing an intake form saves clients two minutes but creates fifteen minutes of staff reconstruction and more errors per case. | Compare the entire journey, including client burden, quality, and staff work; retain or redesign data collection according to the actual objective. | Calling fewer visible steps a system improvement. |
| Genuine complexity | Two independent delivery routes prevent a common outage from stopping an essential service. | Recognize the reliability function and evaluate the routes’ actual independence and cost. | Deleting redundancy solely to lower component count. |
| Wrong speed measure | Drafting takes two minutes, then requests wait two days for one approver. | Identify the approval delay and what makes it necessary; improving draft speed alone has little delivery effect. | Reporting a fast local task as a large end-to-end speedup. |
| Stable automation | A necessary daily conversion has known inputs, outputs, validation, and recovery; its manual operation consumes substantial time. | Briefly establish the earlier decisions and evaluate a useful automation directly. | Requiring lengthy manual repetition or complete upstream redesign despite existing evidence. |
| Changing AI task | A team wants an AI worker for a classification scheme that changes every week; exceptions already dominate the manual work. | Inspect the decision and taxonomy, compare simpler options, and account for supervision and change costs before proposing broad automation. | Equating model capability with a sound workflow or claiming variable tasks can never be automated. |
| Enabling tool | A small script is necessary to measure whether a suspected requirement has any effect. | Use the scoped measurement tool when authorized; distinguish it from committing the production workflow to automation. | Rejecting all automated tools until stage five. |
| Unavailable author | A current requirement’s author left years ago, but a current owner can explain and change it. | Work with the current owner and distinguish current rationale from unknown history. | Making progress depend on recovering an inaccessible original author. |
| Scope and completion | The user asks for an inspection, and the evidence supports retaining the current process. | Report the reasoned result and any decisive uncertainty; make no unrequested changes. | Inventing a redesign, external writes, or an automation just to complete all five steps. |

When revising the skill, record the actual decision it produced, what evidence it used, the case that failed, and the narrow correction. Keep the expected-decision column out of a future independent evaluator’s prompt until after its response.
